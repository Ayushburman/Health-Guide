# Overview

**Ayurveda** (Ayus = Life, Veda = Knowledge) is a traditional Indian system of medicine developed over 3,000–5,000 years ago.

Its central idea is that health depends on maintaining balance among three biological forces known as **Doshas**:

* Vata
* Pitta
* Kapha

Modern science has not validated Dosha theory as a physiological model, but many Ayurvedic herbs have been studied individually using modern research methods.

---

### Charaka Samhita

Focus on:

* Internal medicine
* Diagnosis
* Therapeutics

### Sushruta Samhita

Focus on:

* Surgery
* Anatomy
* Medicinal plants

---

## ~600 CE

### Ashtanga Hridayam

Written by Vagbhata.

Combined Ayurvedic knowledge into a practical medical handbook.

---

## Colonial Period

Traditional medicine lost prominence under British medical systems but later underwent revival and formalization.

# Major Ayurvedic Herbs

## 1. Turmeric (Curcuma longa)

### Evidence Strength: ★★★★★

**Traditional Uses**

* Joint pain
* Wound healing
* Digestion

**Modern Findings**

* Strong anti-inflammatory activity
* May reduce osteoarthritis pain
* Curcumin absorption improves with black pepper

---

## 2. Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)

### Evidence Strength: ★★★★★

**Traditional Uses**

* Strength
* Vitality
* Nervous system support

**Modern Findings**

* Lowers cortisol
* Helps stress management
* May improve sleep quality

---

## 3. Brahmi (Bacopa monnieri)

# Why Some Herbs Work

## 1. Antioxidant Activity

Compounds such as:

* Curcumin
* Vitamin C
* Polyphenols

Help neutralize free radicals.

---

## 2. Stress Hormone Regulation

Ashwagandha may influence the:

* Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis

Resulting in lower cortisol levels.

---

## 3. Antimicrobial Effects

Examples:

* Neem
* Turmeric

Contain compounds that inhibit certain bacteria and fungi.

---

## 4. Improved Bioavailability

Traditional Ayurveda often combines:

* Turmeric
* Black Pepper
* Ghee

Modern research confirms black pepper improves curcumin absorption.
