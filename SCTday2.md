# 1. What Is Breathing?

## Definition

Breathing (Pulmonary Ventilation) is the process of moving air into and out of the lungs to exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.

### Key Functions

#### External Respiration

* Gas exchange in the lungs
* Oxygen enters blood
* Carbon dioxide leaves blood

#### Internal Respiration

* Gas exchange between blood and body cells

#### Cellular Respiration

Cells use oxygen to create energy (ATP).

Formula:

```text
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
```

#### Neural Regulation

Breathing is controlled by:

* Medulla Oblongata
* Pons

The urge to breathe is primarily triggered by:

* Rising CO₂ levels
* Not low oxygen levels

# 2. Respiratory System Anatomy

## Nasal Cavity

Functions:

* Filters air
* Warms air
* Humidifies air
* Produces nitric oxide (NO)

Benefits:

* Better oxygen delivery
* Antimicrobial effects
* Improved airflow

### Recommendation

Prefer nasal breathing over mouth breathing.

---

## Diaphragm

Primary breathing muscle.

During inhalation:

* Contracts
* Moves downward

During exhalation:

* Relaxes
* Moves upward

Ideal breathing:

```text
80% diaphragm
20% chest
```

---

## Alveoli

Tiny air sacs where gas exchange occurs.

Facts:

* ~600 million alveoli
* Surface area ≈ 70 m²
* Oxygen enters blood
* Carbon dioxide exits blood

---

## Intercostal Muscles

Muscles between ribs.

Functions:

* Assist inhalation
* Assist forced exhalation

Excessive chest breathing can reduce efficiency.

---

## Lung Volumes

| Volume                           | Approximate Value |

| Total Lung Capacity              | 6.0 L             |

# 3. Correct Breathing Technique

## Characteristics of Optimal Breathing

* Nasal breathing
* Belly expands during inhale
* Slow and quiet
* Relaxed shoulders
* Longer exhale than inhale

### Ideal Rate

```text
5–6 breaths per minute
```

---

## Dysfunctional Breathing Patterns

Avoid:

* Chronic mouth breathing
* Rapid breathing
* Shoulder lifting
* Chest-dominant breathing
* Stress breath holding

---

## Step-by-Step Correct Breath

### Step 1: Posture

* Sit upright
* Keep spine neutral
* Relax shoulders

### Step 2: Inhale

* Through the nose
* Expand belly

Duration:

```text
4–5 seconds
```

### Step 3: Natural Pause

* Brief effortless pause

Duration:

```text
0.5–1 second
```

### Step 4: Exhale

* Through nose
* Relax belly

Duration:

```text
5–6 seconds
```

### Step 5: Natural Pause

Small pause before next breath.

### Step 6: Repeat

Target:

```text
5–6 breathing cycles per minute
```

# 7. Breath Holding

## Why Breath Holding Feels Difficult

The urge to breathe comes mainly from:

```text
CO₂ buildup
```

not oxygen depletion.

---

## Improving Breath Hold

### 1. Increase CO₂ Tolerance

Practice controlled breath holds.

### 2. Proper Preparation

Use slow diaphragmatic breathing.

Avoid hyperventilation.

### 3. Stay Relaxed

Reduce movement.

### 4. Use Dive Reflex

Cold water on face can help.

### 5. Practice Tables

* CO₂ Tables
* O₂ Tables

---

## Breath Hold Standards

| Hold Time | Level     |
| --------- | --------- |
| <20 sec   | Poor      |
| 20–40 sec | Average   |
| 40–60 sec | Good      |
| >60 sec   | Excellent |

# Key Takeaways

1. Breathe through the nose whenever possible.
2. Use the diaphragm, not the shoulders.
3. Slow breathing improves HRV and relaxation.
